# Survey: explicit top divisions in MGH Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum 1–7 (feasibility of a 2-way vs ≥3-way test)
Blind recording of each editor's stated top, by reading agents, with the AA brief adapted (scratchpad tops_brief_srm.md;
records in data/historic/bedier_srm/tops/). No apparatus is extracted; no OCR is paid for.

## Counting rule (written 2026-11-13 after SRM 1, 2 and 4 had been recorded, before the totals were computed)
A recorded top counts as **explicit**, with k_E = number of top branches, only if all of:
1. filiation_stated is "diagram" or "verbal";
2. confidence is "high" or "medium";
3. not flagged "classes_only" (classes named without their relation), "recensions" (redactions, not one tradition),
   "editor_undecided" or "several_archetypes";
4. k_E ≥ 2.
Everything else is "implied/undecided" and is reported apart, as under A3 for AA.
Feasibility threshold: a pooled AA + SRM test (Fisher, supported vs not, k_E = 2 vs ≥ 3) needs ≥ 8 classified texts
with k_E ≥ 3; at the AA survival rate (14 of 39 explicit tops classified) that needs about 22 explicit k_E ≥ 3 tops.

## Result (all seven volumes recorded; rule applied once)
168 texts recorded in SRM 1–7. Explicit tops under the rule: **51 two-way, 2 three-way** (Arbeo's Vita Haimhrammi,
diagram garbled in the OCR, contamination flagged; Stephen's Vita Landiberti). The other 115 have no stated filiation,
a single manuscript, or a top that is classes only, recensions, low confidence or undecided.
With AA 1–15 (39 two-way, 2 multi-branch explicit): **90 two-way against 4 multi-branch, 96% two-way**, in 239 texts.
Counting every recorded top however tentative (lenient, not the rule): 95 two-way, 31 with three or more branches in SRM; most of the 31 are classes named without their relation.

## Verdict
The pre-registered two-way vs ≥ 3-way test is **not feasible** on the MGH: four explicit multi-branch tops exist in
two series, against the ≥ 22 needed. The survey itself measures Bédier's observation on a second series: explicit
multi-branch tops are as rare in Krusch and Levison as in the Auctores antiquissimi. Where these editors see more than
two groups, they mostly name classes without stating how they relate (flag classes_only), which the rule does not count.

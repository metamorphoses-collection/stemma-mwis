# Later editions survey: did the top of the MGH stemmata change? (2026-11-13)
Literature survey only. No apparatus extracted, no Bédier data, results, code or paper touched. Follow-up to
RESULTS_BEDIER_20261113.md and SURVEY_BEDIER_SRM_20261113.md.

**Scope.** 94 texts with an explicit editor's top: 41 in MGH AA 1–15 (classification_A3 = explicit) and 53 in MGH
SRM 1–7 (the SRM counting rule). MGH tops are taken from data/historic/bedier/tops and data/historic/bedier_srm/tops. All are
two-way except Eugenius carmina (AA, 6 groups), Vita Haimhrammi and Stephen's Vita Landiberti (SRM, 3) and the
one-branch Romana.

**Method.** Seven reading agents searched the web, one per batch: catalogues (zenon, hbz, Brepols, publisher pages),
geschichtsquellen.de, BMCR, Persée, Plekos, archive.org, publisher previews and open-access chapters. Each agent was
told to list only editions confirmed by a hit and never to state a later top from memory. Each claim about a later top
carries one of three access levels: **read in the edition** (the stemma or preface text itself was read), **from a
review or secondary source** (the source is named), or **not accessible**. I re-checked the key 2→≥3 case of
Fortunatus (BMCR) myself. Several review sites blocked automated reading (Heidelberg journals, academia.edu, Cairn,
Google Books, Cambridge); where that happened the entry says so. "No later critical edition" means a search of the
standard places found none. For the minor SRM vitae that search was 2–3 queries each, not a full BHL/Mirabile check.
Reprints of the MGH text (CCSL 115, CCSL 117, Croke 1995, Dräger 2011) and translations are not counted as later
critical editions.

**Changed** = yes if a later critical edition, or in two cases a published stemmatic study, puts the top differently.
That covers a different number of branches, different membership of the branches, or no stemma at all. Unknown = no
later critical edition, or its top could not be read or matched to the MGH sigla.

The rows add up to 94. Outcomes for the 18 priority texts: 1 confirmed 2→3 (Getica), 1 probable 2→≥3 (Vita
Martini), 2 → no stemma (Claudian), 3 regrouped with k = 2 (Paulus, Sidonius × 2), 3 kept (Eugippius, Isidore HG,
Victor Vitensis), 8 unknown.

**Coincidence with new manuscripts.** Every change found comes with a manuscript base larger than the MGH's:
- **Getica:** the new N (Palermo, s. VIII/IX) enters family c, and Q and T are added.
- **Vita Martini:** Kay adds Z (Bodl. Auct. T. 2. 25). But the change, no common node over P, FT and X, comes from
  re-reading the known witnesses, not from Z.
- **Paulus:** Crivellucci adds H2, N2 and A1–A3. M changes sides on the readings of the known witnesses.
- **Sidonius:** Dolveck has 116 manuscripts against Lütjohann's 86.
- **Claudian:** Hall uses far more manuscripts than Birt.

Among the texts kept at k = 2, Petschenig's new W for Victor Vitensis is the one witness that pulled an editor towards
three classes. Change is not driven by new witnesses alone: in Getica and Paulus, the regrouping or the third branch
rests on re-weighing shared readings, notably A with the third family in Getica.

**Reading for the paper.** The Getica remains the one fully documented case of a two-way MGH top undone into three
families. Vita Martini (Kay 2020) and Aldhelm's prose De virginitate (Gwara 2001) are the two candidates worth reading in
print: Kay pp. 23–35 and Gwara pp. 77*–187*. Three of our Bédier verdicts can be set against these changes:
- **Vita Martini:** our verdict was *contradicted* (PN 75 crosses Leo's PTF | NBLMS), and Kay removes Leo's node over
  P, FT and X.
- **Getica:** a pilot verdict of *contradicted* (AOBXYZ), and Grillone separates OB from the HPVL branch.
- **Paulus, Historia Romana:** our verdict was *contradicted* (NZM 48 joins M to N Z), and Crivellucci 1914 moves M to
  the N side.

So the three later revisions of contradicted tops fall where our apparatus test found a crossing family. The pattern is
not clean, though. Dolveck's Sidonius regrouping, with C against the rest, also cuts through families we found stable
and *supported*: TCFP in the carmina and MTCFP in the epistulae. Eugippius and Victor Vitensis, both supported, are kept
by later editors. This is exploratory and observed after the fact, on a handful of cases.

**Limits.** 81 of 94 rows are unknown, mostly because no later critical edition exists (51). Of the 30 later editions
whose top we could not read, most are print-only Budé or CCSL volumes; a library visit would settle them. The 2→≥3
count is therefore a lower bound.

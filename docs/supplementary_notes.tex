\section{Proofs and technical remarks}
\label{app:proofs}
\subsection*{Definitions}
Throughout, $\X$ is the set of surviving witnesses, $|\X|=n$.

\begin{definition}[Collation]
A \emph{collation} of $\X$ is a finite set $U$ of \emph{variation units}. Each unit $u\in U$ has a set $O_u\subseteq\X$ of witnesses legible at $u$, a finite set $R_u$ of readings, and a map $r_u:O_u\to R_u$ assigning to each legible witness its reading. The collation is \emph{complete} if $O_u=\X$ for every $u$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Split]
A \emph{split} of a set $Y\subseteq\X$ is an unordered bipartition $A|B$ with $A\cup B=Y$ and $A\cap B=\emptyset$. It is \emph{full} if $Y=\X$ and \emph{partial} otherwise, and \emph{non-trivial} if $|A|,|B|\ge2$.
\end{definition}

The paragraph of the main text that explains how a collation proposes families reads, in formal terms: at unit $u$, each reading $\rho\in R_u$ proposes the split $\sigma_{u,\rho}=r_u^{-1}(\rho)\,|\,O_u\setminus r_u^{-1}(\rho)$ of $O_u$; the \emph{candidate splits} are the non-trivial splits so proposed, identical splits (same sides, same domain) merged, and the weight $w(\sigma)$ of a candidate is the number of distinct units that propose it (Remark~\ref{rem:double}).

\begin{definition}[Stemma]
An (unrooted) \emph{stemma} on $\X$ is a tree $T$ whose leaves are labelled one-to-one by $\X$ and whose internal vertices have degree at least three. Removing an edge $e$ separates the leaves into a split $\sigma_e$ of $\X$; we write $\Sigma(T)$ for the set of non-trivial edge splits. A \emph{rooted} stemma has, in addition, a distinguished vertex, the archetype; each vertex then defines a \emph{cluster}, the set of witnesses below it.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility and conflict graph]
Two full splits $A|B$ and $C|D$ are \emph{compatible} if at least one of $A\cap C$, $A\cap D$, $B\cap C$, $B\cap D$ is empty, and \emph{in conflict} otherwise. Two partial splits $A|B$ of $Y$ and $C|D$ of $Z$ are \emph{overlap-compatible} if their restrictions to $Y\cap Z$ are compatible. The \emph{conflict graph} $G=(V,E)$ of a collation has one vertex per candidate split, weighted by $w$, and an edge between every pair in conflict (overlap conflict for partial splits).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Buneman~\citep{Buneman1971}; see~\citet{SempleSteel2003}]\label{sprop:buneman}
A set $S$ of non-trivial full splits of $\X$ equals $\Sigma(T)$ for some stemma $T$ if and only if its splits are pairwise compatible; $T$ is then unique.
\end{proposition}

\subsection*{The rooted variant}
The rooted variant is described in main-text Sec.~2.2; formally, each unit $u$ has a reference reading $\rho^*_u$, and the candidates are \emph{clusters}: sets $C=r_u^{-1}(\rho)$ with $\rho\neq\rho^*_u$ and $|C|\ge2$. Two clusters are compatible if they are disjoint or nested (a laminar pair), the weight of $C$ is the number of units at which it departs together, and selection is again~(1), now on the conflict graph of clusters. A laminar family is the cluster set of a unique rooted tree, rooted at the reference. To compare with splits, add a formal reference leaf $r$ that belongs to no cluster. Each cluster $C$ then corresponds to the split $C\,|\,(\mathcal{X}\cup\{r\})\setminus C$, and since every complement contains $r$, two such splits are compatible exactly when the clusters are disjoint or nested. Without $r$ the equivalence fails: $ABC$ and $BCD$ cross as clusters, yet their splits on $\{A,B,C,D\}$ are compatible. The leaf $r$ stands for the reference text as a whole; it is not the reference reading $\rho^*_u$, which is chosen afresh at each unit. With it, the rooted problem is the split problem on $\mathcal{X}\cup\{r\}$, except that a cluster whose complement is a single witness, such as $\{B,M,P\}$ against $E$ in Fig.~4, is meaningful as a cluster though trivial as a split.

\subsection*{Further remarks}

\begin{remark}[Double counting]\label{rem:double}
For a binary unit, both readings propose the same bipartition. Counting each reading inflates the weight of binary units by a factor two; in the Donatus data this affected about 700 of 2{,}468 reading-level proposals. All numbers in the paper and in these notes use one count per unit.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Multistate units]
Turning a unit into ``reading against the rest'' splits is not the same as multistate character compatibility: a three-state unit can be convex on a tree without each of its reading-vs-rest splits being an edge split of that tree. Native multistate incompatibility is, like joint completability (S2), a hypergraph condition.
\end{remark}

The results on complexity, consistency and certificates used in the main text (Sec.~2.2 and Sec.~4.4) are proved, and verified in Lean, in the public formalisation~\citep{StemmaLean}. Two splits proposed by the same unit never conflict, and a matching argument gives deterministic certificates of uniqueness that can be checked on a collation. On four witnesses there is a textual long-branch zone, which shrinks by the factor $\alpha^2$ when only shared departures count, where $\alpha$ is the probability that two independent errors at the same place take the same reading.

\section{Fragmentary witnesses}
\label{app:partial}
The main text (Sec.~2.3) states these results in prose; here they are stated formally. Propositions~\ref{sprop:buneman}, \ref{sprop:triple} and~\ref{sthm:cutting} are proved and verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib in the public formalisation~\citep{StemmaLean}, the last with the solver and the search for an infeasible core treated abstractly.

\begin{definition}[Joint completability]
A family of partial splits is \emph{jointly completable} if each can be extended to a full split of $\X$ so that the extensions are pairwise compatible.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Pairs are exact, triples are not]\label{sprop:triple}
(a) Any two partial splits that are compatible on their common witnesses are jointly completable.
(b) Three partial splits can be pairwise compatible on their common witnesses without being jointly completable; on five witnesses, $p=12|34$ (observed on $1234$), $q=12|35$ (on $1235$) and $r=14|25$ (on $1245$) are such a triple.
(c) Five witnesses is the minimum: no such triple exists on four.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact selection by cutting planes]\label{sthm:cutting}
Iterate: solve~(1) on the overlap-conflict graph together with the cuts found so far; test whether the optimum $S$ is jointly completable; if not, find a non-completable $K\subseteq S$ and add the cut
\begin{equation*}
\textstyle\sum_{\sigma\in K}y_\sigma\le|K|-1 .
\end{equation*}
The procedure terminates, and its first jointly completable optimum is a maximum-weight jointly completable family.
\end{proposition}

The partial splits $p,q,r$ are quartets, and (b) is an instance of a minimal incompatible quartet set~\citep{Shutters2013}. Statements (a) and (c) were also checked exhaustively by computer for up to six witnesses; there are 200 obstructing triples on five. Feasibility is therefore a hypergraph condition, but only through triples and larger sets. The correction is a standard lazy constraint generation; its interest here lies in the encoding and in the fact that it is fast on real traditions.

The completability test is a satisfiability problem. We orient every split away from a reference witness (whose side is itself a variable where it is unobserved), introduce Boolean side variables only for unobserved witnesses, and require for every pair one of the three laminar relations (disjoint, nested either way); CP-SAT~\citep{ORTools} solves it with one assumption literal per split, so that an infeasible run returns an infeasible core $K$, not necessarily minimal. By Proposition~\ref{sprop:triple}(a), every infeasible core contains at least three splits. The size of the correction on Heinrichi and Donatus is given in main-text Sec.~3.1.

\section{Generative language models}
\label{app:llm}
The protocol, the results (Table~9) and the check of the evidence the models cite (Table~10) are in main-text Appendix~C; this section adds details. Each prompt explained that the symbols of the matrix are arbitrary labels and that `?' marks a lacuna, and asked for the families as nested sets in a fixed JSON format; families that cross were to be reported as invalid, but every answer that could be parsed was a valid tree. Each model ran with a generous output limit, and a call that returned no answer was retried once.

Claude Fable 5.2 took fifteen to twenty minutes and about $10^5$ output tokens per run. More reasoning did not give better trees: Sonnet answers in about three thousand output tokens and scores 63, and the open-weight models that spend close to $10^5$ score lower; several exhaust their output limit while reasoning and return nothing. No other model reaches the exact methods in any of its runs.

The claims and their checks are in the folder \texttt{data/llm\_results/evidence\_checks} of the code archive~\citep{StemmaMWIS}. The best trees come with mostly true but undiagnostic evidence (Fable: typically lacunae that other witnesses share too, and on Donatus hardly anything that can be checked), while the most reliable citations come with one of the weakest trees (Grok): the quality of the reasons and the quality of the tree show no relation in these answers. This follows from the task. A stemma rests on tallying which witnesses share readings across tens of thousands of cells and on choosing the heaviest set of mutually compatible groups, an exact aggregation and search that a language model performs in text, with no reliable counter and no check that its families are jointly optimal, while its justifications are never verified against the matrix. The exact method does this by construction, at a cost of seconds to minutes, reproducibly under fixed solver settings; where several optima exist, as on both benchmarks, their range is reported.

\section{The Getica in detail}
\label{app:getica}
Because the format is regular, the apparatus can be read by a fixed parser, with no language model and every unit traceable to its line: 4{,}611 units on the Getica. Witnesses that a page lists in parentheses or with a line range (Y, Z and the epitome S) are treated as known only where cited; the others agree with the lemma unless cited. Checked against the printed page, the parser recovers the variant groups of the apparatus entry by entry.

Families can be counted in two ways. Unrooted, as in the benchmarks of Sec.~3.1, any split of the witnesses counts, including agreement in Mommsen's text. Rooted (Sec.~2.2), only groups that share a \emph{departure} from a reference reading count. A departure from the edited text is not automatically an error: the lemma is Mommsen's choice, made with his own stemma in mind, and rooting by it can carry that stemma into the result. Each unit is therefore also rooted at its \emph{majority} reading, which needs no editorial choice; in the 4{,}413 units where one reading is in the majority and the edited reading is identified, it coincides with Mommsen's lemma in 93\%, and the optimum keeps the same families (OB, XYZ, XY, HPV, HPVL, HP at 1.00; SOB 0.85; A+XYZ 0.85).

The main text (Sec.~4.1, Table~3) gives the families and the profile of A. Three details are added here. S shares 56 departures with O and B, 36 of them with O and B alone (the family SOB of Table~3), against one with order I. Unrooted, A sits in order I (with HPVL, stability 0.96); rooted, its strongest family is A+XYZ (0.90 by the lemma, 0.85 by the majority reading). The profile of mixed affiliation does not by itself fix the direction: an order~I text corrected from manuscripts of the other orders and a descendant of P later contaminated from them would both produce it. What it excludes is the simple picture of A as an uncontaminated member of order~I, and it lists the readings on which the direction can be decided.

The Ambrosianus is digitised and was read at five of the places on which the argument rests. In three, A has the reading Mommsen reports for AXYZ in the first hand and in the line, not as a later correction: \emph{in prima Scythie sede iuxta meotidem paludem} (p.~64), \emph{Post haec non longi temporis intervallo} (p.~89, with the abbreviation for \emph{haec} identified from the same scribe's \emph{inter haec}) and \emph{lactis nutrimenta suscipiant} (p.~91). The control place, \emph{Gothus subsensit pertimuit} (p.~95), confirms the agreement of A with P. At the fifth place (p.~84) the abbreviation cannot be resolved between \emph{quod} and \emph{quam} at the available resolution. On the two pages read in full, every report of A that could be compared matches the manuscript, which also carries interlinear corrections by a second hand, outside Mommsen's apparatus by design. Since the AXYZ readings stand in the first hand, the mixture is older than the correction of A: it was in its exemplar or made by its scribe (Mommsen's account of A is in his prooemium, pp.~L--LI and n.~88). One of the three (\emph{paludem}, which the preceding sentence supplies) is the kind of agreement a scribe can produce alone; the others are not.

The later editions, built on a Palermo manuscript unknown to Mommsen~\citep{GiuntaGrillone1991,Grillone2017}, keep his three families and enlarge the third. Antonino Grillone's stemma (pp.~XXXIV--XXXV) has the three families descend from the archetype directly, with no common ancestor for orders I and II. Mommsen's own apparatus contains almost no evidence for that ancestor: orders I and II, as wholes, depart together from his text in 2 of the 4{,}587 units whose edited reading is identified (of 4{,}611), against 4 for orders I and III and 167 for orders II and III (A set aside, S counted with order II where present). The 167 are places where his text sides with order I against the other two (in 124 of them every witness of order I present has the edited reading), which is the reliance on the first family that Grillone criticises (p.~XXXIII). Grillone does not derive A from P: he gives it a branch of its own beside the first family, with a line of contamination from B, because of the good readings it has alone or with the third family and B (pp.~XXXIII--XXXIV). Grillone's edition was read only after these results. The two accounts of A are close but not the same claim. Grillone makes A an independent, contaminated witness. The method, from Mommsen's evidence of 1882, finds it of mixed affiliation, which challenges an exclusively first-order affiliation but does not establish independence from P: the first-hand readings exclude correction by A's later corrector, not contamination when A or its exemplar was copied.

\section{Extraction of the \emph{Monumenta} apparatus}
\label{app:extraction}
The four editions and the extractor are introduced in main-text Sec.~4.2. These apparatus have no page headers of witnesses, so the extractor keeps only groups that share a departure from the edited text, for which absent witnesses need not be known. On the Getica it returns the same eight families as the dedicated parser, at stabilities within a few points, and one more of weight 2 at stability about 0.5. Every group that disagreed with an editor was checked against the raw entries; two such disagreements were OCR artefacts (a capitalised \emph{AD} in poem titles, a corrector's mark read as a siglum) and were removed. The extraction is measured on twelve printed pages (three per edition), transcribed from the page images by an AI agent and not checked by a human reader, on which every group of witnesses sharing a departure is listed, 200 in all. The extractor recovers 84\% of them (169 of 200), with precision of at least 90\%, against 68\% without the repairs these pages exposed (hand marks rendered by the OCR as \emph{!} or \emph{?}, confusions such as \emph{l} for \emph{I}, sigla split by a space, an apparatus block cut at a garbled line). Since these pages guided the repairs, the figures are development figures, not estimates on unseen pages (most of the remaining false positives are correct groups of the following page). A fresh OCR of all 2{,}308 pages from the 300-dpi scans with Tesseract, and a general Transkribus model on the test pages, did no better overall (72\% and 56\%) and introduced systematic misreadings of italic sigla, so the tables use the repaired extractor on the original OCR. Missed groups do not invent evidence, but omissions need not be random, so they can shift relative support between families. The \emph{Variae} are run in three parts, since Mommsen's six classes each cover only some of the twelve books.

\section{The series test in detail}
\label{app:series}
Main-text Appendix~B lists the fourteen texts of the test with their verdicts. This section gives the admission of texts, the thresholds and the verdict rule, the preregistration, the robustness checks, the exploratory checks, the later editions text by text and the details of Bédier's count.

Of the 71 texts of the \emph{Auctores antiquissimi} recorded from the prefaces and printed stemmata, 20 have no stemma and 5 fewer than two sigla (46 remain: 36 explicit tops, 5 implied, 5 undecided); 4 cannot be located in the page images (42); 14 have too few witnesses or too few units, among them the only two multi-branch tops (28); 7 fail the proof page after one repair round on fresh pages, which six others passed (21); and of these 4 are pilot or case-study editions, 2 have an undecided top and 1 an implied one (14). Branches of one witness are not classified, so in six texts the verdict rests on one branch; a division is contradicted if any classified branch is, supported if all are, and silent otherwise. Two to seven stable families per text could cross a branch, among 8 to 193 candidates; those that do are AC and ACV (Prosper), BVL and BVLT (Ennodius), PN (\emph{Vita Martini}) and NZM (Paul).

The admission thresholds of Sec.~4.3 were chosen as follows. Four is the smallest number of witnesses for which a family can cross a top division in the unrooted coding (with three, every split is trivial); in the rooted coding of shared departures, which the test uses, three would suffice, since clusters such as AB and AC cross, so the bound of four is a choice that keeps both codings on the same footing. Thirty citations keep witnesses that are fragmentary or cited only in passing, whose silence in the apparatus says little, out of the analysis. Two hundred units is the order of magnitude at which a family of five to ten units, the weight that decides a top, can reach bootstrap stability 0.90 (the 75 units of the Lawrence prologue leave its lower node unresolved, Sec.~3.3).

Two exploratory checks point to how the dichotomies arise. First, shared departures that link two of the data's well-attested groups are, at best, evenly split between the editor's branches and links across them. In the texts examined, which include the case-study editions, where the editor's division captures them above chance (the \emph{Variae}, Isidore's chronicle, Prosper, the Getica; $p\le0.03$ against divisions of the same sizes), about as many still cross it, and in five texts it does no better than a random division (in Claudian's \emph{carmina maiora}, worse). The top is chosen among alternatives of comparable weight. Second, where one branch departs as a whole and the other is intact, the edited text follows one preferred branch in 58--90\% of such places (median 78\%, ten texts with two branches of at least two witnesses), not always the larger. Both fit Bédier's own account. In his reading, editors first sort the manuscripts into ``plusieurs familles, trois ou plus'', then simplify the scheme while establishing the text; a three-branch scheme decides by majority, mechanically, and by breaking one branch the editor recovers part of the authority he had given away~\citep[pp.~172--173]{Bedier1928}. The thin, balanced evidence at the top and the preferred branch are what such pruning would leave in the data.

The protocol was fixed on 13 November 2026, before any text was classified, in the project's private repository; the record has no third-party timestamp. It fixes the question, the reference (shared departures from the edited text, silence reported as silence), the thresholds, the verdict rule and the statistic. Its amendments, all made the same day before the first classification, followed failed proof pages, apart from the exception for Prosper. The random-division and Weitzman comparisons, the perturbation analysis and the controls below were not registered. The 67 gold pages were transcribed by AI agents and not checked by a human reader. The 4{,}091 pages of the located texts were transcribed by a vision model chosen on the twelve test pages. Texts that failed their proof page had one repair round: nine were repaired, diagnosed on development pages only and scored once on fresh proof pages, and six then passed: four of the fourteen (Isidore's \emph{Chronica maiora}, Eugippius, Victor, Ennodius), the \emph{Variae}, a case-study edition, and Avitus' \emph{carmina}, whose top the editor leaves undecided. Seven texts were excluded at the proof page, three of them after the repair. Prosper is admitted as a stated exception to the location threshold (start match 0.749 against 0.75). The perturbation analysis is \texttt{code/historic/bedier\_sensitivity.py} (5{,}600 classifications, all solved to optimality): in every condition the editors' divisions remain far better than random ones (pooled $p\le2\times10^{-3}$, and $\le2\times10^{-4}$ outside the most adverse case), and at least five of the fourteen tops stay unresolved. The comparison with divisions that cross no stable family is \texttt{code/historic/review\_bedier.py} (pooled over twelve texts, $p=1$).

The perturbation conditions are random loss of 16\% and 30\% of the extracted groups, 10\% and 20\% spurious groups, losses concentrated on crossing or on agreeing groups, and restoration of missed groups, 40 replicates each; twelve of the fourteen verdicts are unchanged in at least 98\% of replicates. Marcellinus, supported at stability 0.91 on 219 units, falls below the admission gate under any loss of 16\%, and the errors that matter move tops towards silence or contradiction. Counted from the majority reading, no text is supported, which the coding forces since every supported branch has more than half the witnesses, and six are contradicted (the four of the main text, Marcellinus and the \emph{Historia Brittonum}). Without departures that differ from their lemma only in spelling (11.6\% of departures; 39 of 40 removals read by eye are spellings) all verdicts stand; a broader filter (21.6\%, about a third of whose removals are morphological) turns Ennodius silent, its family BVL falling from 211 to 85 departures. Against the divisions with the editor's branch sizes that cross no stable family, the editor's division ties in nine texts and does worse in three; none exists for Paul. This comparison also protects stable families that are candidate tops, so no compared division can be contradicted. Holding fixed only the stable families that lie inside another stable family, the editors' divisions score 13 against a mean of 7.97 (one-sided $p=0.008$, exact convolution over 13 texts).

Later scholarship has revised some of these tops, but gives no validation either way. Of the fourteen tested texts, nine have a later top that could be compared (surveyed after the test, with uneven access). Contradicted in the test and revised later: the \emph{Vita Martini}, where Kay's tentative stemma has five branches below the archetype, P, FT, X, the newly added Z and a group of N, LBE and SMU, so that Leo's branch PTF no longer has a common node~\citep[p.~34]{Kay2020}, and Paul, whose later edition moves M to the side of N, as the crossing family NZM does here~\citep{Crivellucci1914}. Supported and revised: Sidonius' poems, which a later study regroups with C against the rest, across the supported family. Contradicted and kept: Prosper, whose later editors retain Mommsen's classification, placing C, which carries the crossing family AC, among the contaminated manuscripts, and read the two recensions AOR and MYL as possibly Prosper's own editions of 445 and 455~\citep[50--52]{BeckerKoetter2016}. Supported and kept: Eugippius and Victor. Silent: Claudian's two texts, whose later editor gives up a stemma for an open, contaminated tradition~\citep[x--xi]{Hall1985}, and Isidore's Gothic history, whose top is kept. Unknown: Ennodius, Isidore's chronicle and the \emph{Historia Brittonum}; no later edition: Marcellinus and the \emph{Romana}. Outside the test, the Getica, contradicted by the same rule, is revised~\citep{Grillone2017}, and so are Sidonius' letters, by the same later study. Across all 94 explicit tops 8 are known to have changed, 9 to have been kept, 26 have a later edition not yet read, and 51 have never been re-edited, which is too few for a test.

The count of two-way tops (main-text Sec.~4.3) uses one rule for both series, written before the totals were computed. A top counts as explicit if its filiation is stated in a diagram or in words with high or medium confidence, is not limited to classes, recensions or several archetypes, and has at least two branches. The \emph{Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum} (168 texts) have 53 such tops, 51 two-way; the \emph{Auctores antiquissimi} 41, 39 two-way, in the verified recording (34 under the rule applied literally to the raw recording, which gives 83 two-way tops of 87 in the two series). The 36 explicit tops of the series test come from a third recording, made under its own admission rule. Krusch, Levison and Mommsen drew 70 of the 94. Weitzman's figures are probabilities for one tradition, so they give no test of an editor's set of tops, whose traditions share the editor's practice. Under the literal rule four of the fourteen test texts (Sidonius' poems, Isidore's chronicle, the \emph{Historia Brittonum}, the \emph{Romana}) would not count as explicit. Krusch drew 31 two-way tops of 34, Levison 20 of 20 and Mommsen 16 of 16; counted one vote per editor, the mean two-way share is 0.98 (under the literal rule 15 editors, $p=0.020$ against 77\%; \texttt{code/historic/review\_weitzman.py}). Four multi-branch tops are too few to compare with the two-way ones. Unlike most phylogenetic programs, the exact optimum does not require binary output, and it returns a top of three or more branches whenever no family joins them; but it shares a tendency to over-resolve, discussed in main-text Sec.~6, so a two-way top is not by itself evidence of editorial preference. The count also bears on an open question. Roelli's impression from printed stemmata was that bifid stemmata are much less prevalent in classical and medieval Latin editions~\citep[p.~218]{Roelli2020}; in the two \emph{Monumenta} series counted here they are as prevalent as in Bédier's sample (105 of 110). Weitzman's prediction applies to traditions with a lost archetype and at least two surviving lines, and the binomial comparison takes his rates as given.

\section{Thegan in detail}
\label{app:thegan}
The tree and the two rootings are given in main-text Sec.~4.5. Against Tremp's text the root lies on W's own edge, his top division of W against all the others (43 shared departures); against the majority it lies between W, T, P and the other four (35). The two passages that decide it, found only in W, P and T, are a clause in c.~36 and two sentences against Archbishop Ebo in c.~44; Tremp judges the first authentic from Thegan's stance towards the empress Judith, the second from Ebo's situation and the passage's style~\citep[pp.~42--43]{Tremp1995}.

Tremp set S (Schaffhausen, Min.~75) apart with B2, a reworked text his apparatus does not use; the map joins S to the Reims pair L2 B4 on 26 readings, and all 26 stand in S's main text, in both of its hands, uncorrected. This establishes a substantial Reims affiliation and excludes later correction of S as the explanation at those places; it does not distinguish descent from contamination already present in S's exemplar or introduced as S was copied. If its link to B2 holds, the two sit with the Reims pair together. The stable pair B3 Pi also bears on the first edition: of the 175 readings the apparatus gives to Pi alone, 136 could be checked in the 1588 print; 133 stand there and three are slips of the apparatus. Tremp derives Pi and B3 from the same exemplar, or Pi from a close relative of it~\citep[pp.~48, 52]{Tremp1995}, and B3, read at 155 of these places, never shares Pi's reading: B3 normalises the names, so Pi's medieval forms (\emph{Ludeuuicus}, \emph{hardiati}) are the exemplar's, while whether Pi's other singular readings are its manuscript's or Pithou's own needs a third witness of the branch. In either case the pair stands, since it rests on the readings the two share.

\section{Supplementary discussion}
\label{app:discussion}
The method can also be placed in the history of the debate it touches. After 1928 the field divided: biblical scholars turned to Quentin's classification by all variants, editors of Old French largely to Bédier's single best manuscript, while Italian scholars, from Giorgio Pasquali (1885--1952) to the neo-Lachmannians, refined the genealogical method with the history of each tradition, the study of contamination and, more recently, models of loss that explain why most surviving stemmata divide in two~\citep{Roelli2020,Trovato2017,GuidiTrovato2004,Weitzman1987}. Main-text Sec.~6 sets out how the method separates these positions into steps that can each be checked.

Main-text Sec.~6 also sets out how best-supported reconstruction turns a three-way branching into a two-way one. Further sources bear on it. Timpanaro saw that if every coincidence is explained by a common lost exemplar ``the majority becomes a totality''~\citep[pp.~180--182]{Timpanaro2005}; Haugen called it a force of dichotomy inherent in recension~\citep[pp.~606--608]{Haugen2016}; and Eklund defended dichotomy as the safer error for an edition, since a false third branch outvotes authentic readings~\citep[pp.~37--38, n.~30]{Reeve2011}. The explanation by loss, that survival alone makes most stemmata two-branched, goes back to Castellani~\citep{GuidiTrovato2004,Hoenen2017}. The birth--death comparison of~\citet{Camps2025} sets editors' reconstructions against true trees, so it cannot by itself tell whether reconstruction is biased. Simulated on the same model, best-supported reconstruction overshoots the editors' root share without a threshold; admitting a family only on two to five shared departures, it reproduces that share and their resolved triples, though not their internal polytomies~\citep{StemmaMWIS}. The level of support that chance agreements reach is not known in advance; a rule whose null is the coincidence rate measured on the collation itself removes most false pairings~\citep{StemmaMWIS}. In the \emph{Lai} every rival family is lighter than the weakest of the four, but only by one reading (DEF, 24, against EF, 23), and the population margin is not certified at 95\%. Fourquet named Bédier's paradox~\citep{Fourquet1945}, and Bédier's own case has since been re-examined at length~\citep{Baker2018,Duval2021}.

Contamination, the main addition of the neo-Lachmannians, is not yet modelled, but the evidence map flags it. A strong family that the optimum leaves out marks a conflict, and deleting one witness at a time shows which witness carries it. In an exploratory check, deleting Vogel's B in Ennodius removes 48\% of the conflict weight (families of at least five units that cross the optimum), although only 34\% of the family weight involves B; B is the leading suspect in 89 of 100 bootstrap replicates. In the \emph{Getica} no single witness carries more than its share: A and Z lead equally, which fits mixture between the families rather than in one manuscript, although A is the witness that Grillone links to B by contamination (S4).

Main-text Sec.~6 states the limits; details are added here. The language-model scores vary by up to twelve points between runs, and models change quickly. In machine collations, correlated transcription error behaves like kinship: on a nine-witness tradition (Richard of St Victor) the two noisiest transcriptions form a heavy false family once noise units are admitted. The coding matters. Counting every reading, as the benchmarks of Sec.~3 do, groups two corrupted copies whenever the others keep the original reading; counting only shared departures from a reference, Lachmann's rule, groups them only when their errors coincide, which shrinks the zone in which more text does not help by the factor $\alpha^2$ (S1)~\citep{StemmaLean}. On the two benchmarks the rooted coding of Sec.~2.2 changes little (Heinrichi 81.5, inside the range 79.1--82.3 of the unrooted optima; Donatus 8 of the editor's 12 families, within the 8 to 10 of the unrooted optima; \texttt{code/benchmark/rooted\_variant.py}), as expected where coincidence is frequent: in Heinrichi, two unrelated copies that depart at the same place agree about eight times in ten~\citep{StemmaMWIS}. A few rules of coding follow. A scribal act counts once. A lacuna stays a lacuna. Transcription noise is left out, but not spelling as such~\citep{AndrewsMace2013}, and filters by type of variant do not remove chance agreement, which an edition's own errors can even create~\citep{Schmid2004}. The reference is the archetype's text where it can be told from the author's~\citep[p.~39]{Reeve2011}, chosen independently of the stemma under test. Any weighting by significance is fixed in advance, since on artificial traditions human judgements of significance did no better than chance~\citep{Andrews2016}.

Three problems remain open: choosing that level of support from the data, a model of which readings are prone to coincidence, and an exact treatment of contamination, which split networks already display descriptively~\citep{BryantMoulton2004,HusonBryant2006}. We propose an encoding that would stay within the MWIS, not yet implemented or validated: a suspect witness becomes two leaves, one per exemplar, as Paolo Trovato, a leading neo-Lachmannian, gives a witness two sigla, A$'$ and A$''$, for the sections copied from different exemplars~\citep[p.~122]{Roelli2020}, each family that contains it has two versions joined by a conflict edge, and a penalty per split keeps the tree from splitting every witness; whether it recovers known cases of contamination remains to be tested.

Stemmata have been drawn from apparatuses before, as the Germanist Hermann Paul did for Hartmann von Aue's \emph{Iwein} from Lachmann's in 1874~\citep[pp.~470--471]{Roelli2020}; what changes is the scale, and with it what the method helps with.

Main-text Sec.~6 lists what the evidence map offers editors and the field; some points are added here. Collation can go where it changes the tree. Readers of editions, historians or philologists using a text, can see which of the editor's groups the readings establish and which rest on judgement, and weigh a variant accordingly. The audit of a series is cheap: transcription and extraction cost about \$0.015 a page here. New editions can go further: automatic collation~\citep{Dekker2015}, handwritten text recognition and the digital stemmatology surveyed in~\citet{Roelli2020} already take a tradition from images to a matrix of readings, and an exact optimum with its evidence map closes the loop to a stemma whose every branch can be checked, with the editor's judgement applied where the map shows the evidence is thin. On quantum hardware, arrays of about a thousand atoms~\citep{Lim2026}, given per-atom detunings, might reach thresholded instances of a tradition such as Richard of St Victor's, provided thresholding is checked not to change the optimum.

\section{Texts, editions and baselines}
\label{app:texts}
Every tradition used, with the edition it is read from, is listed in main-text Table~6, and the fourteen texts of the series test in Table~8. Unless stated otherwise the data are the edition's apparatus, not a fresh collation, and sigla are the editor's.

\subsection{Witnesses}
The seven copies of the Lawrence prologue are described in main-text Appendix~A (Table~7). The bifolium L was reused in 1561 as the cover of a Florentine lawsuit file (Fig.~1a, b); line ends hidden by the turn-ins were read on photographs taken with the folds lifted.

Mommsen's eleven witnesses of the \emph{Getica} are H (Heidelberg), P (Palatinus), V (Valenciennes), L (Laurentianus), A (Milan, Ambrosiana C~72 inf., 11th--12th century), the epitome S, O (Ottobonianus), B (Breslau), X (Cambridge), Y (Berlin) and Z (the lost Atrebatensis, cited from testimony).

The Priscus fragments rest on sixteenth-century copies of a tenth-century codex that burned at the Escorial in 1671; de Boor groups B, M and P under a lost intermediate X and M, P under a lost parent $y$.

Thegan's witnesses include S (Schaffhausen, Min.~75) and Pi, Pithou's first edition of 1588, printed from a manuscript now lost. Its apparatus was read from the page images and checked against an independent OCR.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{app:baselines}
The benchmarks are described in main-text Sec.~3.1. The skeleton criterion is the average sign distance over witness triples, with the outliers Da, I and J excluded, as by the organisers. The summary table of~\citet{RoosHeikkila2009} reports different values for the same methods (neighbour joining 64.4, parsimony 74.4, best method RHM 76.0). The published scoring reproduces the organisers' 67.887 for their file, not 64.4, so the two sets of numbers are on different scales; all comparisons here are internal, and RHM is rerun rather than quoted. On Donatus, the family sigla of the apparatus are expanded to their members as Licciardello defines them. Without the expansion, with the members of a group siglum treated as unknown, only 957 units remain informative and much of the evidence is lost. All methods receive the same expanded input, but its effect on their rankings need not be the same; the comparison is one of consistency with the edition, not of independent reconstruction accuracy.

The baselines are maximum parsimony (phangorn ratchet~\citep{Schliep2011}), neighbour joining on co-observed distances, the method of Camps and Cafiero~\citep{CampsCafiero2015}, a split network (Neighbor-Net~\citep{BryantMoulton2004} in phangorn, on Hamming distances with pairwise deletion of lacunae; the few pairs of fragments never observed together get the mean distance), and Bayesian inference (MrBayes~\citep{Ronquist2012}, Mk model with gamma rate variation; readings ranked beyond the tenth in a unit, 91 of 33{,}108 observed cells on Heinrichi and 3 of 62{,}374 on Donatus, are set to missing, as the program allows ten states).

\emph{Neighbor-Net}: splits kept in decreasing weight if compatible with those already kept. \emph{MrBayes}: Mk model with gamma rates, two runs of four chains, majority-rule consensus; on Heinrichi the runs had not converged after $4\times10^6$ generations (average standard deviation of split frequencies 0.13, against 0.014 on Donatus), but the consensus is the same at $10^6$ and $4\times10^6$, a nearly flat posterior like the exact method's several optima. \emph{Roelli--Bachmann}: rebuilt from~\citet{RoelliBachmann2010}\citep[pp.~326, 345]{Roelli2020}: presence--absence characters weighted by their pairwise compatibility, weighted distances, Fitch--Margoliash tree (weights $1/d^2$) by nearest-neighbour interchanges from neighbour joining; the down-weighting of syntactic runs, which needs a word alignment, is omitted; with equal weights it scores 66.1 and 9. It uses pairwise compatibility to weight variant locations, not to select families, and its weighting adds 2.5 points to the same distance tree without it.

\emph{RHM} and \emph{Semstem}: the authors' code (Stemweb repository). RHM was compiled standalone, with one repair (it read compressed lengths through a zlib structure that current zlib no longer exposes, so every cost was zero); three runs of $10^5$ annealing steps, the two that reach the best cost scoring 83.1. Semstem was ported mechanically from Python~2, search unchanged, three seeds of up to 200 iterations; witnesses it places at internal nodes are grouped with their descendants for scoring. RHM compares copies by compressing their text, so on Heinrichi it reads the organisers' word-by-word alignment, while every other method reads the coded matrix derived from it. The matrix keeps who agrees with whom, not that \emph{dominus} is closer to \emph{domini} than to \emph{rex}, so RHM's result compares a different input as well as a different algorithm. On Donatus only apparatus states are available, and filling unreported passages with the edited text would make witnesses look more alike than the evidence shows.

A family-by-family comparison on Heinrichi explains why exact compatibility and parsimony score alike: the true tree has 17 non-trivial families; the twelve optima of MWIS select 32 to 34 families, and parsimony 34; in the optimum compared family by family, seven wrong families are shared with the parsimony tree and five true families are missed by both, and ten of its wrong families form a single nested chain: one large group of the true tree is resolved as a ladder, its witnesses added one or two at a time, instead of into its true subfamilies. Three alternative unit weightings designed to discount homoplasy-prone units (inverse number of readings, minority readings only, binary units only) did not clearly improve on one count per unit (80.7, 81.3 and 71.9, against 79.1--82.3 for the optimal trees under one count per unit).

\section{Quantum runs in detail}
\label{app:quantumruns}
This section gives the run details summarised in main-text Sec.~5.2 and Appendix~D.

Priscus is the smallest native instance found in which the unweighted and weighted problems differ. The apparatus of de Boor's edition of the \emph{Excerpta de legationibus}, for the Roman embassies and restricted to the four witnesses E, B, M and P (S9.1), yields five candidate families with six conflicts (Fig.~4): B+M+P, his lost intermediate X (45 shared departures), M+P, a second lost copy (8), and E+B (4), E+P (3), E+M+P (3). The maximum-weight set, $\{$B+M+P, M+P$\}$ (weight 53), is de Boor's stemma exactly; since he left unrecorded the slips his stemma explained, this checks consistency with the editor, not independence from him. Without weights, four sets of two families tie as maximum. Emulation means computing the sweep numerically at the processor's geometry, either for an ideal device (\emph{noiseless}) or with the manufacturer's error model of readout errors and dephasing (\emph{noisy}).

On Ruby, the 3{,}500 Priscus shots were taken in four passes, each giving one outcome per copy, so 31{,}500 copy outcomes (correlated within a shot, so each pass is also checked on its own). In every pass 89\% of copy outcomes are valid independent sets and 62\% are maximum independent sets (51\% predicted with the noise model; 0.125 at random); each of the four maximum sets appears in 11--24\%, de Boor's stemma at 16\%. In the weighted emulation at the setting run (one copy, effective weight $0.3+0.7\,w/w_{\max}$, \texttt{ruby\_mis\_prep.json}), the probability of de Boor's stemma rises from 0.22 to 0.57 noiseless and from 0.12 to 0.19 noisy, against 1/32 for a random configuration.

The \emph{Lai de l'Ombre} register keeps the families supported by at least eleven units: seven families and six conflicts, placed eight times in a 56-atom register. It was built from an earlier extraction of the apparatus (DE 43 rather than 41), which gives the same tree. Four maximum sets of four families tie, and the maximum-weight set is unique: $\{$AB, CG, DE, DEF$\}$. On the device 89\% of copy outcomes are valid independent sets and 33\% are maximum ones (21\% predicted with the noise model); the four maximum sets appear in 4--11\%, the tree most often, but by a narrow margin over the next (11\% against 10\%). The Lawrence core (the thresholded graph of main-text Sec.~5.3) has a unique maximum set, the optimum's stemma (Fig.~3): 95\% of outcomes are valid, and 31\% return the stemma (14\% predicted with noise; 1/32 at random), against 8\% for its rival. On all three instances the device exceeds the noise model on the share of maximum sets; on conflict-free outcomes it is slightly below it for Priscus and the \emph{Lai} and matches it for Lawrence.

On the Lawrence core the optimum keeps four families (B+F+Mi+P+R, B+R, B+F+P+R, B+F+R; weight 21) and its rival three (B+F+Mi+P+R, P+R, B+F+P+R; weight 18); the hardware returned the optimum in 2{,}181 outcomes and the rival in 587. Without weights the device sees only the size of a set, so this preference reflects the larger set, not the supports.

Classical baselines and uncertainties. The graphs run have 5 vertices and 6 edges (Priscus), 7 and 6 (\emph{Lai}) and 5 and 2 (Lawrence), with 4, 4 and 1 maximum sets among 10, 40 and 20 independent sets. A random-order greedy maximal set is maximum with probability 1, 1 and 2/3, a uniform independent set with probability 0.40, 0.10 and 0.05, a uniformly random configuration with 0.125, 0.031 and 0.031; the hardware gives 0.616, 0.328 and 0.312. With the copies of one shot as a cluster the standard errors are 0.003, 0.006 and 0.006 (design effects 1.1--1.3), and a test of homogeneity rejects equal shares across copy positions in all three runs (maximum-set shares 0.56--0.65, 0.28--0.37, 0.23--0.38). The four Priscus passes agree (0.599--0.620). The emulations are density-matrix solutions on one copy (QutipEmulator, 4{,}000 samples per setting; noise: readout false positives 0.02 and false negatives 0.10, dephasing at rate $1/4.5\,\mu$s); they predicted conflict-free shares of 0.926, 0.933 and 0.952, against 0.893, 0.893 and 0.949 on hardware.

Weights and residual interactions. Compressing the weights is checked on the end-of-sweep energy of one copy with all residual interactions between atoms, enumerated over every configuration. On the registers run on Ruby, the weighted lowest state is de Boor's stemma for Priscus (gap 2.0 rad\,$\mu$s$^{-1}$ at floor 0.3); for the \emph{Lai} it is the maximum-weight set at floor 0.3, by a narrow gap (0.03), but drops AB at lower floors; for the Lawrence core it is not the optimum, since the residual interactions on B+F+R exceed its local detuning and the set without it lies lower. New layouts for the \emph{Lai} and Lawrence, with conflicting pairs within $0.90\,R_b$ and compatible ones at $1.26\,R_b$ or more, have the maximum-weight sets as weighted lowest states (gaps 2.4 and 1.9 rad\,$\mu$s$^{-1}$ at floor 0.3). Without weights the residual interactions also break the ties between maximum sets: for Priscus they favour \{MP, EMP\}, the set the hardware returns most often (24\%), with de Boor's stemma second (16\%).

A separate sweep study (\texttt{data/ruby/analysis/sampler\_test/} of the code archive) uses the same drive and the same weight map, on the Priscus register and on the new layouts, with the sweep time and the weight floor varied (one copy, 4{,}000 samples per setting and the exact final distribution). Its figures are shares among maximal outcomes, whereas the 0.57 above is a share among all outcomes (at 6{,}000~ns and floor 0.3, $0.79\times0.72$ of outcomes maximal). On an ideal device, at floor 0.3, the share of the best-supported history among maximal outcomes rises with the sweep time (Priscus 0.45 to 0.79, \emph{Lai} 0.37 to 0.60, Lawrence 0.87 to 0.95 between 1{,}000 and 6{,}000~ns), and the competing histories come out largely in the order of their support (rank correlation 1.0 on the \emph{Lai}, 0.8 on Priscus). Under the noise model the best history remains the most frequent outcome of the emulation. Scripts and outputs: \texttt{data/ruby/analysis/} of the code archive.
